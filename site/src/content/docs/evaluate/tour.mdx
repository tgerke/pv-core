Fifteen screens follow one idea through the product: **the app never asks anyone to update
a status.** People record facts (a report arrived, a physician assessed causality, a form was
sent, a regulator acknowledged it) and every state on every page, including whether a case
is reportable and when each report is due, is computed from those facts each time the page
loads. The screens come from the seeded demo, so several cases stand in for one: each step
names the case it shows. Use the arrow keys or the buttons to move; "Show all steps" lays
the whole tour out on one page.

<WalkStep
  id="step-queue"
  title="The morning queue"
  image={queue}
  alt="The case queue as the CRO administrator: ten cases across two sponsors, overdue clocks first, four stat tiles, and filter chips for each derived state.">

Dana Whitfield, the CRO administrator, sees every case across both sponsors. Overdue
obligations sort first; the tiles count what needs attention today. Each row shows the
case's derived state, its expedited class (7-day, 15-day, or not expedited, with the
reason), the next due date, and how many obligations are open or overdue.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-new-case"
  title="A serious event arrives"
  image={newCasePartial}
  alt="The new-case form with the study, patient initials, subject number, and one event entered; the Valid ICSR rail shows two of four criteria met and the button reads Save as intake.">

A site sends an SAE form. Marcus Lee, case processor, opens **New case** and enters what
arrived. The rail on the right tracks the four minimum criteria for a valid Individual Case
Safety Report and updates as he types. With a patient and an event but no reporter and no
suspect drug, the case would be saved as an **intake** item: visible in the queue, fully
editable, and without a regulatory clock.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-valid"
  title="Four criteria met"
  image={newCaseValid}
  alt="The same form after adding the reporter's family name and qualification and the suspect study drug; all four checks are green and the button reads Create case.">

Add the reporter's name and the suspect drug and the button changes to **Create case**.
The clock starts the day the sponsor first knows the case meets the criteria, recorded as
the version's awareness date (day zero). It defaults to the date the information was
received; when the two differ, the app requires a rationale and the audit trail keeps it.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-intake"
  title="An intake item from the EDC"
  image={caseIntake}
  alt="Case US-CORC-2026-0009 pushed in by the EDC: the version card carries an amber notice that the case is below the E2B(R3) minimum criteria, missing an identifiable reporter.">

Cases also arrive by machine. US-CORC-2026-0009 was pushed by the EDC through an intake
identity that can create cases and read nothing back. It came without a reporter, so it
sits in intake with a notice naming what is missing; the payload it arrived with is kept
verbatim on the case, with its hash, so the provenance of every field is inspectable.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-coding"
  title="Coding and the three judgments"
  image={eventsTab}
  alt="The Events tab of US-CORC-2026-0002: the reported German term, its MedDRA path from LLT to SOC, the hospitalization criterion, dates and outcome, and chips reading serious, unexpected against IB v2.0 §6.3, reporter related, sponsor related.">

Reportability rests on three judgments per event: is it serious, is it unexpected, and is
it reasonably related to the study drug. The event is coded to a Lowest Level Term from
the loaded dictionary and the app records the full path. Seriousness is six checkboxes on
the event. Expectedness is derived against the reference safety information in effect when
the event occurred; here the term was not listed in the Investigator's Brochure version in
force, so the event is unexpected. Causality is recorded per drug and event for both the
reporter and the sponsor. Together they make US-CORC-2026-0002 a 15-day SUSAR.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-assessments"
  title="Causality, drug by event by assessor"
  image={assessmentsTab}
  alt="The Assessments grid of US-CORC-2026-0001: one row per suspect drug, event, and assessor, with related yes or no, method, result, rechallenge, and an expectedness override column.">

Assessments live in a grid of drug × event × assessor. The reporter's row and the
sponsor's are separate; each records whether a reasonable possibility of a causal
relationship exists, plus the method, the result wording, and any rechallenge information.
Until the sponsor has assessed an event, the app treats it as related: the fail-safe
direction is to over-report. The safety physician can override the derived expectedness in
either direction, and the override must carry a rationale.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-obligations"
  title="The clocks appear"
  image={obligationsCard}
  alt="The Obligations panel of US-CORC-2026-0002: three rows for the EU CTR SUSAR 15-day rule, the FDA IND 15-day rule, and the investigator notification letter, each with day zero, a due date five days past, and an Overdue chip.">

The moment a case is valid, the app materializes every obligation the reporting rules
imply, each with its day zero and due date. Rules are data an administrator keeps: a
destination, a timeline in calendar days, and a test over the case. For this case three
rules fire, one to EudraVigilance, one to the FDA, one to the study's investigators. Their
status is derived on every read; here all three are overdue, and the record-submission
button stays disabled until the version carries an approval signature.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-why"
  title="Why does this rule apply?"
  image={ruleMatchesCard}
  alt="The Rule matches panel listing each rule that applies to version 1, its destination, the kind of report, the timeline in days, and day zero.">

"Why does this rule apply" is a click away: the rule matches panel shows the predicate the
engine evaluated for the version, with the rule's citation next to it. When an evaluator
asks how a due date was arrived at, this is the answer, and it is the same answer a year
later because rules are never edited in place; the old one is ended and a new one added.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-sign"
  title="Medical review and signature"
  image={dialogSign}
  alt="The Sign medical review dialog: it explains that the signature records name, time, and meaning bound to the hash of this exact version, asks Priya Raman to re-authenticate, shows the version hash, and offers Confirm identity and sign.">

When data entry is complete the case goes to medical review. Priya Raman MD reviews the
sections and assessments, then signs. Signing asks her to re-authenticate; the signature
records who, when, why, and the cryptographic hash of the exact version signed. The first
signature locks the version: anything that must change afterwards is a new version.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-approved"
  title="Approved and locked"
  image={caseApproved}
  alt="Case US-CORC-2026-0001 approved: the header shows 7-day, fatal, unblinded, and next due in three days; the version card shows v1 with a lock; the action bar offers Record submission and Open follow-up.">

US-CORC-2026-0001 is a fatal, unexpected, related event and its 7-day clocks are due in
three days. It carries both a medical-review signature and an approval, each shown with
the hash it signed and whether the version still hashes to the same value. The version is
locked; the actions left are to record what was sent or to open a follow-up.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-record"
  title="Recording what was sent"
  image={dialogSubmission}
  alt="The Record submission dialog: destination FDA CDER preselected, kind initial, format with CIOMS I PDF, MedWatch 3500A PDF, E2B(R3) JSON, portal, and email as options, a payload attachment selector, message id and note.">

Sending happens outside pv-core (a gateway, a portal, an email); recording it happens
here. Choose the destination, the kind of report, and the format. For CIOMS I, Form FDA
3500A, and the E2B(R3) JSON format the app renders the document itself from the approved
version and stores the exact bytes; for anything else attach the file you sent. The
database refuses a submission unless the version carries an approval signature bound to
its current hash.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-cioms"
  title="The rendered CIOMS I"
  image={cioms1}
  alt="Page one of the CIOMS I form rendered from US-CORC-2026-0001: reaction information, suspect drug, concomitant drugs, manufacturer information, and a footer naming the version and its SHA-256 hash.">

The CIOMS I and 3500A renderings follow the official forms' field lists and name the
version hash on every page, so what was sent can always be traced to what was signed. The
footer says it plainly: a rendering is not the record; the signed version hash is.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-acknowledged"
  title="Acknowledged"
  image={submissionsCard}
  alt="The Submissions panel of US-CORC-2026-0006: three submissions of version 1, to the FDA as CIOMS I with a CA acknowledgement, to EudraVigilance, and to the investigators, each with a payload download and the version hash that was sent.">

US-CORC-2026-0006 went out three ways. Each submission row names the destination, the
format, who sent it and when, the payload bytes (one click to download exactly what was
sent), and the version hash that was current at the time. The FDA row carries its
acknowledgement; the others show "no acknowledgement" until someone records one.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-follow-up"
  title="New information: a follow-up"
  image={caseFollowUp}
  alt="Case US-CORC-2026-0006 with version 2 selected: a follow-up in data entry, the version toggles showing v1 locked and v2 open, and a fresh 15-day obligation next to the initial one that keeps its history.">

Three days ago new information arrived on the same case. Opening a follow-up clones the
approved version; the processor enters what changed, and a new follow-up obligation
appears with its own day zero while the initial obligation keeps its history (submitted,
on time or not). The locked v1 stays exactly as it was signed and sent.

</WalkStep>
<WalkStep
  id="step-reporting"
  title="Across studies, by due date"
  image={reporting}
  alt="The Reporting page: expected submissions across every study grouped by due date, with status chips, filter chips for overdue, due soon, pending, submitted, acknowledged, superseded, and waived, and record-submission actions on open rows.">

The Reporting page is the same information across studies, grouped by due date and
filterable by status, with on-time metrics per study and destination below. The same
content goes out as a plain-text email once a day (or however often the team schedules it)
to everyone whose grant covers the study; the digest stores nothing and reads the record
at send time. There is an example [further down](#what-the-record-can-produce).

</WalkStep>
</Walkthrough>

## Scenarios the clock has to survive

Real caseloads are mostly edge cases. The seed stages the ones evaluators ask about first.

    unexpected event that would start a clock and flag the case in the queue; on this
    non-serious one it stays a visible reminder that the physician's judgment is still owed.

## Who sees what

Access is a grant on a person: a role scoped to a sponsor organization, to a single study,
or to the whole instance. A CRO hosts several sponsors on one instance and their staff never
see each other's cases. The header shows who you are; the app renders only the actions your
grants permit. The same queue, as each of the demo personas:

## Not pictured

There is no gateway transmission, no WHODrug coding of concomitant medications, no
schema-valid E2B(R3) XML, no SAE reconciliation against the EDC, and no QC step distinct
from medical review. Which of those matter for your engagement is what the
[fit assessment](/pv-core/evaluate/fit/) is for; the [roadmap](/pv-core/roadmap/) says how
far each one is.
